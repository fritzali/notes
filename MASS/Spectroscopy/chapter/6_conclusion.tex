\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The spectroscopic study of \xray emitting plasma in galaxy clusters yields valuable insights into the evolution of cosmic structures.
Classifying systems into CC and NCC categories enables us to effectively reconstruct distinct thermodynamic histories. Furthermore,
analyzing thermal bremsstrahlung and discrete line emission serves as a reliable diagnostic tool for determining ICM properties,
including local temperature and electron density, as well as entropy and gas pressure. 

At the same time, the purely gravitational baseline model is faced with some clear observational contradictions. The predicted catastrophic
cooling flows for relaxed clusters are completely absent, requiring heating mechanisms like AGN feedback and galactic winds to stabilize
cores. Additionally, deriving radial profiles in three dimensions from projected two dimensional spectral maps is complicated even further by
lingering uncertainties regarding nonthermal support. 

Modern \xray missions, including XRISM and NewAthena, are deploying highly sensitive cryogenic microcalorimeters with excellent spectral
resolution expected to resolve such uncertainties. These instruments will rigorously map core enrichment dynamics and potentially detect the faint
WHIM \xrays along the bridges of cosmic filaments.
