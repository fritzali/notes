## 1. The dominant stable phase of ISM that corresponds to HII regions is:

* **a)** cold
* **b)** warm neutral
* **c)** warm ionized
* **d)** hot

**Correct Answer:** **c) warm ionized**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> HII regions are environments comprised predominantly of ionized hydrogen ($\text{H}^+$) surrounded by hot, massive UV-emitting stars. This state directly corresponds to the **Warm Ionized Medium (WIM)** phase of the interstellar medium, which typically maintains temperatures around $T \sim 8000\text{ K}$.

---

## 2. Magnetic field in ISM obtained directly from intensity of synchrotron radiation can be estimated from:

* **a)** Zeeman effect
* **b)** equipartition calculation
* **c)** rotation measure
* **d)** Stark effect

**Correct Answer:** **b) equipartition calculation**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> Synchrotron radiation intensity depends simultaneously on two unknown variables: the cosmic ray electron density and the strength of the magnetic field. To isolate the magnetic field strength from intensity data alone, astrophysicists use the **equipartition calculation** (or minimum energy assumption), which posits that the energy density of cosmic rays is roughly equal to the energy density of the magnetic field.

## 3. Write formula for column density of HI:

**Correct Answer:** 

$$N_{\text{HI}} = 1.822 \times 10^{18} \int T_B(v) \, dv \quad \left[\text{cm}^{-2}\right]$$

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> In the optically thin limit, the column density of neutral hydrogen ($N_{\text{HI}}$) is linearly proportional to the velocity-integrated brightness temperature ($\int T_B(v) \, dv$) of the landmark 21-cm hyperfine spin-flip transition line, where $T_B$ is measured in Kelvin and $v$ is in $\text{km/s}$.

## 4. For the formation of $\text{H}_2$ molecules in ISM the necessary catalyst are:

* **a)** ionized Fe atoms
* **b)** neutral Fe atoms
* **c)** $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ molecules
* **d)** dust grains

**Correct Answer:** **d) dust grains**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> Direct gas-phase recombination of two neutral hydrogen atoms ($\text{H} + \text{H} \rightarrow \text{H}_2 + \gamma$) is incredibly inefficient because the temporary molecule cannot easily shed its binding energy via a radiative transition. Interstellar **dust grains** act as physical catalysts; hydrogen atoms adhere to the grain surfaces, migrate, encounter one another to form $\text{H}_2$, and then desorb back into the gas phase using the excess reaction energy.

## 5. What processes are mainly responsible for creation of molecular lines in the near IR:

* **a)** rotational transitions
* **b)** vibrational transitions
* **c)** electronic transitions
* **d)** bound-free transitions

**Correct Answer:** **b) vibrational transitions**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> Molecular transitions spanning different energy scales dictate specific spectral regimes. Pure rotational changes possess low energy and emit in the radio/sub-millimeter bands, while electronic transitions carry high energy and map to the UV/optical bands. **Vibrational transitions** (specifically ro-vibrational transitions) require intermediate energy levels that map precisely to near- and mid-infrared (IR) wavelengths.

## 6. Production of 511 keV $\gamma$-line is result of:

* **a)** leptonic interactions
* **b)** hadronic interactions
* **c)** de excitation of heavy nuclei
* **d)** annihilation

**Correct Answer:** **d) annihilation**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> The 511 keV gamma-ray line is the classic signature of **electron-positron ($e^- + e^+$) annihilation**. Because the rest mass energy ($m_e c^2$) of both an electron and a positron is exactly 511 keV, their mutual annihilation at rest converts their mass into two monochromatic 511 keV photons flying in opposite directions.

## 7. The dust grains mainly responsible for extinction in optical are:

* **a)** big dust grains
* **b)** small dust grains
* **c)** big molecules
* **d)** AIBs

**Correct Answer:** **a) big dust grains**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> Dust particles absorb and scatter light most effectively when their physical radius is comparable to the incoming wavelength. Optical light spans roughly 0.4 to 0.7 microns. Therefore, **"big" dust grains** (mostly silicates and carbonaceous particles with radii between $0.1 - 0.25 \, \mu\text{m}$) drive the bulk of interstellar extinction and reddening in the visible spectrum.

## 8. The smallest number of Galactic cosmic rays should be at energies (among four energies given):

* **a)** 10 MeV/n
* **b)** 100 MeV/n
* **c)** 10 GeV/n
* **d)** 100 GeV/n

**Correct Answer:** **d) 100 GeV/n**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> The energy spectrum of Galactic cosmic rays follows an incredibly steep, downward-sloping power-law distribution ($dN/dE \propto E^{-\alpha}$, where $\alpha \approx 2.7$). Because the flux drops off drastically as particle energy scales upward, the highest energy option listed—**100 GeV/n**—will feature the lowest absolute abundance of cosmic ray particles.

## 9. The emission of lines from metals in higher ionized states can be expected from:

* **a)** dense cores of molecular clouds
* **b)** photodissociation regions
* **c)** hot bubbles
* **d)** emission nebulae

**Correct Answer:** **c) hot bubbles**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> Highly ionized metal ions (such as O VI or Fe XIV) require massive kinetic inputs for collisional ionization, meaning they can only exist in ultra-high temperature zones ($T \sim 10^5 - 10^6\text{ K}$). These criteria are met within **hot bubbles** (the Hot Ionized Medium) carved out by violent supernova remnants and stellar winds. Normal emission nebulae lack the hard radiation fields or temperatures to support these extreme states.

## 10. The important ISM radio recombination line is:

* **a)** H92$\alpha$
* **b)** K$\alpha$
* **c)** [Blank option]
* **d)** $^{12}\text{CO}$

**Correct Answer:** **a) H92$\alpha$**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> **H92$\alpha$** is a classic radio recombination line corresponding to a electronic transition from $n=93$ to $n=92$ in a highly excited hydrogen atom (a Rydberg atom). Because the energy gaps between these massive principle quantum numbers are tiny, the emitted photon falls cleanly into the radio spectrum ($\sim 8.3\text{ GHz}$). Conversely, K$\alpha$ belongs to the X-ray regime, and CO tracks molecular rotational transitions.

## 11. The main prerequisite of heating in ISM is:

* **a)** existence of relativistic electrons
* **b)** existence of suprathermal electrons
* **c)** existence of cosmic ray protons
* **d)** deposition of energy in high velocity clouds

**Correct Answer:** **b) existence of suprathermal electrons**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> Dominant interstellar heating pathways—including the dust grain photoelectric effect, X-ray ionization, and cosmic ray ionization—operate by liberating free electrons into the gas with immense kinetic profiles. These are called **suprathermal electrons** because their energies vastly exceed the thermal energy distribution of the ambient gas. As they scatter and experience elastic collisions, they disperse this extra energy, successfully heating the surrounding medium.

## 12. Cooling of ISM is fundamentally based on:

* **a)** hydrodynamic dissipation
* **b)** elastic collisions between particles in the system
* **c)** magnetic field compression
* **d)** inelastic collisions between particles in the system

**Correct Answer:** **d) inelastic collisions between particles in the system**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> Interstellar gas drops in temperature when kinetic energy is converted into radiation that can freely escape the system. This conversion relies on **inelastic collisions**, where a kinetic particle (like an electron or atom) collides with an ion, atom, or molecule and transfers its kinetic energy into exciting the target's internal electronic, vibrational, or rotational states. The target then decays back to its ground state by spitting out a photon that leaves the optically thin cloud, resulting in net cooling.

## 13. The stable phase of ISM are defined for constant:

* **e)** temperature
* **f)** density
* **g)** pressure
* **h)** entropy

**Correct Answer:** **g) pressure**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> Classic interstellar multi-phase models (such as the FGH or McKee-Ostriker models) demonstrate that different phases of gas (like the Cold Neutral Medium and Warm Neutral Medium) coexist alongside each other in dynamic equilibrium. For these distinct densities and temperatures to exist side-by-side without imploding or expanding out of control, they must remain in **thermal pressure equilibrium** ($P_{\text{thermal}} = \text{constant}$).

## 14. The ejecta from the exploded star in an SNR should exist in the:

* **a)** free expansion phase
* **b)** Sedov phase
* **c)** radiative phase
* **d)** dissipation phase

**Correct Answer:** **a) free expansion phase**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> In the initial **free expansion phase** of a Supernova Remnant (SNR), the mass of the swept-up ambient interstellar medium is tiny compared to the sheer mass of the stellar explosion debris ($M_{\text{swept}} \ll M_{\text{ejecta}}$). Thus, the blast expanding outward is dominated almost purely by the unhindered star-stuff ejecta itself before ambient matter takes over the dynamics during the subsequent Sedov-Taylor phase.

## 15. Write down equation for compression on radiative shocks:

**Correct Answer:** 

$$\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \mathcal{M}^2$$

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> In a radiative shock, the gas heated by the initial shock front quickly cools via radiation back down to its pre-shock temperature profile. Because the system mimics an isothermal process across the broader shock zone, the density compression ratio ($\rho_2/\rho_1$) is not bound to a maximum limit of 4 (like standard adiabatic shocks) but is instead directly proportional to the square of the shock's sonic Mach number ($\mathcal{M}$).

## 16. Which kind of ionization front is opposite to shock waves in a sense of compression:

* **a)** R-type ionization front
* **b)** it does not exist
* **c)** D-type ionization front
* **d)** all types of ionization fronts

**Correct Answer:** **c) D-type ionization front**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> Shock waves are defined by strong gas compression where density jumps significantly ($\rho_2 > \rho_1$). Across a **D-type (Dense) ionization front**, the front moves slowly through high-density neutral gas, causing the material to ionize and violently *expand* into a lower-density state ($\rho_2 < \rho_1$). Because it forces a strong rarefaction/expansion of the medium passing through it, it acts opposite to a compression shock.

## 17. The Larmor radius for a charged particle is defined by equation:

**Correct Answer:** 

$$r_L = \frac{p_\perp}{|q|B} = \frac{\gamma m v_\perp}{|q|B}$$

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> The Larmor radius (or gyroradius) dictates the physical radius of a charged particle's helical orbit around a magnetic field line. It is mathematically governed by the particle's perpendicular momentum ($p_\perp$), its net electrical charge ($q$), and the surrounding magnetic field strength ($B$).

## 18. Fermi acceleration mechanisms are based on:

* **a)** interaction between stationary magnetic fields and charge particles
* **b)** interaction between moving shock discontinuities and neutral particles
* **c)** interaction between electric fields and charge particles
* **d)** interaction between moving magnetic fields and charge particles

**Correct Answer:** **d) interaction between moving magnetic fields and charge particles**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> Fermi acceleration (spanning both First-order and Second-order variants) accelerates cosmic rays through the **interaction between moving magnetic fields and charged particles**. Particles magnetically scatter off moving irregularities (like Alfvén waves or magnetized clouds). Because these magnetic mirrors are moving, the particles experience a Doppler-like energy shift, yielding a systematic net energy gain over time.

---

## 19. Show expression for energy increase in every interaction between test particle and magnetic perturbation in DSA theory:

**Correct Answer:** 

$$\frac{\Delta E}{E} = \frac{4}{3} \frac{u_1 - u_2}{c}$$

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> In Diffusive Shock Acceleration (DSA), a particle bounces back and forth across a non-relativistic shock front via magnetic scattering networks. Every full round-trip across the shock results in a systematic fractional energy gain ($\Delta E/E$) proportional to the first power of the velocity jump ($u_1 - u_2$) divided by the speed of light ($c$). This linear dependency makes it a highly efficient "First-order" Fermi process.

## 20. The energy index expected from the Fermi acceleration theory, should be steeper if efficiency for acceleration of particles is:

* **a)** higher
* **b)** lower
* **c)** moderate
* **d)** these are independent parameters

**Correct Answer:** **b) lower**

> ### Explanation
> 
> 
> The power-law spectrum of accelerated cosmic rays is represented as $dN/dE \propto E^{-s}$, where $s$ is the spectral or energy index. A **steeper** spectrum corresponds to a higher numerical value of $s$, meaning high-energy particles are scarce. The index $s$ is governed by a balance between how fast particles gain energy versus how quickly they escape the acceleration zone. If the acceleration efficiency is **lower**, particles cannot reach ultra-high energies before escaping, causing the resulting spectrum to flatten out less and look much steeper.
